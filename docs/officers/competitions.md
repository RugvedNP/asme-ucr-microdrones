## Progression

| Stage | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Test flights** | Fall wk 10 | Indoor, per pair, just getting airborne |
| **Internal time trials** | Winter | Simple gate course, pairs compete against the clock |
| **Internal scrimmage** | Late Winter | Bracketed, all pairs, low stakes — the practice run |
| **Inter-school** | Spring | UC Davis / UCLA — travel or host |
| **Hosted event** | Spring, stretch | Our own competition, if capacity allows |

Running internal events first is what makes the external one survivable. The
first time you discover your course design doesn't work should not be with
another school watching.

## Obstacle course

Design constraints for indoor microdrones:

- Gates large enough for beginners — 60–80cm openings, not racing-tight
- Netting or a bounded room; assume drones will leave the course
- Nothing fragile in the flight volume
- A clear, marked **kill zone** where a pilot can safely land or crash
- Course reset must take under a minute or the event drags

## FPV transition

FPV is a real step up in both cost and risk, and it belongs after members can
already fly line-of-sight.

Prerequisites before anyone flies FPV:
- Comfortable flying the Winter brushless build line-of-sight
- A designated **spotter** for every FPV flight — the pilot cannot see the
  room
- Goggles and video gear budgeted (see [parts-list.md](parts-list.md))

## Hosting our own event

The stretch goal. Only attempt after we've *attended* someone else's, so we
know what we're signing up for.

Needs, roughly: venue booking, a written ruleset, registration and check-in,
safety marshals, a pit area, spare-parts support for visiting teams, and
sponsor visibility — pit displays and banners are an explicit sponsor benefit
in the packet, and a hosted event is the best place to deliver on it.
